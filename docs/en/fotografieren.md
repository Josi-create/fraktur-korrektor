# Photographing books yourself

Many archives, reading rooms and special collections do not allow scanners, but they do allow phones. That is
enough: a present-day phone takes pictures from which text recognition gets good results – provided a few things
are right when you take them. Whatever goes wrong while photographing has to be repaired later. A good picture
saves all the after-work.

## The most important point: one page at a time

Photograph **the left page first, then the right one**, each on its own. That is twice as many pictures, but
afterwards there is nothing to split, and every page fills the picture. This is the single most important advice.

If you did end up with double pages, that is no disaster: *Prepare scans* splits them – see
[Reading in a PDF or images](pdf-import.md).

## Camera and book

- **Hold the camera parallel to the page**, not at an angle from above. The easiest way: hold the phone over the
  page and tilt it until the edges of the page run parallel to the edges of the picture.
- **Keep the book as flat as possible.** Follow the rules of the reading room (book cushions, wedges, weights) and
  never force an old book flat. Where a page will not stay down, hold it with a finger **at the outer edge** – it
  does not get in the way of the text there, and *Prepare scans* paints it over in white (tick box *Remove dark
  margins and fingers*).
- **Portrait orientation**, and hold the phone steady. Tap the middle of the page to focus.

## The page fills the picture

Text recognition needs about **300 pixels per inch**. Rule of thumb: a book page 15 cm (6 in) wide needs about
**1800 pixels** across. Any present-day phone manages that easily **if the page fills the picture** – but not if
it sits small in the middle with half the table around it. So go close, until only a narrow margin is left around
the page. Do not zoom in with your fingers: that only enlarges the picture, not the sharpness – move the phone
closer instead.

Small print, footnotes and Fraktur are grateful if you go a little closer still.

## Light

- **Even** – the whole page equally bright, no bright half and no dark half.
- **No flash.** It reflects off smooth paper and is forbidden in many reading rooms anyway.
- **Not in your own shadow.** Leaning over the book often casts a shadow on the page. Stand so that the light
  comes from the side or from the front.
- Light by a window is usually best – but without direct sunlight, which casts hard shadows.

## Order, file format and file names

- **All pages of one book in one folder**, and nothing else in it.
- **The file names must sort in page order.** The phone numbers them consecutively by itself (`IMG_0001.jpg`,
  `IMG_0002.jpg` …); if you photograph in order, that works out. If you took a page twice, delete the worse picture
  before reading them in – otherwise the page appears twice in the book.
- **iPhone: photos as JPEG.** The iPhone usually saves photos in the HEIC format, which Fraktur-Korrektor cannot
  read. So switch **before** photographing: *Settings* → *Camera* → *Formats* → *Most Compatible*. Photos already
  taken are converted to JPEG by the iPhone itself when you transfer them to a Mac or PC by cable, as long as
  *Settings* → *Apps* → *Photos* has *Automatic* (not *Keep Originals*) selected at the very bottom. Android phones
  save JPEG anyway.

To read them in: in the library click **Open …** → **Choose folder …** and point to the folder. The program reads
the images in the order of their names – from there on it works as described in
[Reading in a PDF or images](pdf-import.md).

## Try a sample first

Before you photograph 300 pages in the reading room: **take two or three pages, read them in and look at the
traffic light.** If it is green, carry on as you are. If it is yellow or red, it is almost always one of the
points above – usually the page is too small in the picture, crooked or blurred. That can still be changed now;
after your visit to the archive it cannot.

## If they are double pages after all

- **Prepare scans** (built in, one click) splits double pages, straightens crooked pages and removes dark edges
  and fingers at the margin: [Reading in a PDF or images](pdf-import.md).
- **ScanTailor** also helps with pages that curve towards the spine, with stains and uneven light – a separate
  program with more manual work: [Installing the tools](install-tools.md).

## Book-scanning apps

There are phone apps that split double pages, flatten the curve towards the spine and retouch fingers while you
take the picture – for instance **vFlat Scan** (Android and iPhone). Free to try; for whole books you need a
subscription, in the App Store in September 2026 about 4 to 5 US dollars a month or 36 to 40 a year. The pictures
from such an app can be read in like any other PDF or folder of images.

Bear in mind: the photos pass through another company's app, and whether they leave your phone in the process is
not disclosed by the maker. For archival material you may only photograph under conditions, ask beforehand if in
doubt. Fraktur-Korrektor itself sends nothing to the internet.
